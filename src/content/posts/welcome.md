I want the work I do to have a clear relationship with the life I want to build. That means spending time on problems I care about, contributing something useful and continuing to learn. It also means paying attention to the people, responsibilities and constraints around the work.

This is the direction behind this website. A place to think through problems, explain decisions and keep a record of how my understanding changes.

## My north star

My north star is a fulfilling life and career, with room for curiosity, responsibility and autonomy. I want to develop the judgment to choose worthwhile problems and the ability to follow them through.

For me, that makes direction a practical question. What am I learning? Who benefits from this work? What does it require of me? Is the way I spend my time consistent with what I value?

The answers can change as I gain experience. I want to revisit them deliberately, especially when an opportunity looks attractive before its everyday reality is clear.

## Understanding the problem

My work across data, software and coordination has made me interested in how the parts connect. A technical decision has consequences for the people using a system, the people maintaining it and the people depending on its results.

When I approach a problem, I want to understand that context early. Who encounters it? What are they trying to do? Where does the current process become difficult? Which constraints are fixed, and which assumptions still need checking?

I try to keep observations, interpretations and open questions separate. A plausible explanation is something to investigate. A result needs enough context to understand what was measured and where the conclusion applies.

## Turning a problem into a roadmap

I want a roadmap to explain how the next step will help us make a decision. The sequence I aim to follow is simple:

1. **Define the problem.** Describe the situation, the people affected and the change that would matter. Agree on what a useful outcome would look like and how it could be observed.

2. **Identify the uncertainty.** Gather the available evidence, make assumptions explicit and find the question most likely to change the plan. Check dependencies, costs and practical limits at this stage.

3. **Run a focused experiment.** Choose a manageable step that can answer that question. Decide in advance what result would justify continuing, revising the approach or stopping.

4. **Evaluate the result.** Compare what happened with the intended outcome. Look at failure cases, effort and maintenance needs alongside the main result. Record what remains uncertain.

5. **Choose the next commitment.** Use what was learned to decide how much more time and complexity the problem deserves. Expand the work when the evidence supports it, and keep ownership and ongoing operation part of that decision.

The level of detail should fit the consequences of being wrong. A small, reversible experiment can move quickly. A decision that is costly to undo needs more scrutiny before commitment.

## What I want to make visible

Here, I plan to write about the reasoning behind the work: how a question is framed, which options are considered, what an experiment can establish and why a plan changes. I’ll use public material and self-contained examples, keeping the discussion focused on ideas and methods that can be shared responsibly.

I want each post to leave a reader with something they can examine or use: a clearer question, a trade-off explained, a reproducible example or a limitation worth remembering.

This first post sets out the principles I want to work by. Future posts will be a way to put those principles into practice, revisit them and learn in public.
